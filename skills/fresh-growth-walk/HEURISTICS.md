# Fresh Growth — Heuristics

Numbered rules for judging whether a stranger's arc through the product
is any good. Cite as `§group.rule`. **A finding without a citation isn't
ready** (§M.3).

## Contents

- §0 The evidence floor · §1 Demand (S1 Find) · §2 Message (S2 Learn) ·
  §3 Activation (S3 Sign up, S4 Onboard) · §4 Retention (S5 Use) ·
  §5 Revenue (S6 Pay) · §6 Loops (S7 Grow) · §7 Blueprint & method
- Citing across systems · The line of visibility · Benchmarks ·
  The verdict scale

Every rule is *walkable* — you get a yes or a no by walking the arc, not
by reading the code or the analytics.

The reasoning behind each group lives in its master library:
`references/demand.md` (§1) · `references/message.md` (§2) · `references/activation.md` (§3) · `references/retention.md` (§4) ·
`references/loops.md` (§5, §6) · `references/evidence.md` (§0). §7 is method and belongs to no single
library.

**§0 is the floor, and it is here because of the traffic.** Most products
running this walk cannot support a computed conversion rate, so a growth
system that reaches for one produces confident garbage. §0 exists to make
*"the sample cannot answer this"* a legitimate result rather than a gap —
the direct analogue of fresh-job-walk's *not exercised*, and the local form of
§M.9.

**§7 is cross-cutting** and is cited alongside a domain rule, not instead
of one.

## §0 — The evidence floor

*The rules that stop this system inventing certainty it has not earned.*

| # | Rule | Master |
|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | State the n. A finding drawn from fewer observations than could distinguish it from chance says so, inside the finding — not in a caveat at the end | Kohavi |
| 0.2 | **A send with no stamp is not a send.** A page with no impression is not a page anyone read. The artifact existing is not the artifact arriving | — |
| 0.3 | **"The sample cannot answer this" is a finding, not a gap.** Record it, name what would answer it, move on | Kohavi |
| 0.4 | Small samples give direction, never size. Never convert one walk into a percentage | Hubbard |
| 0.5 | Segment or the average lies. One number across five doors describes none of them | Kaushik |
| 0.6 | Any metric named as a target stops measuring what it measured. This is why nothing here is scored | Goodhart |
| 0.7 | Twyman's law — a surprising number is far more often an error than a discovery. Verify before reporting it | Kohavi |
| 0.8 | **Benchmarks are a stopwatch, not a dashboard.** Compare an observed walk against the band; never compute a rate you did not observe | Kohavi |

## §1 — Demand (S1 Find)

*Does anything of ours reach them at the moment the problem is felt?*

| # | Rule | Master |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Name the moment the problem is felt. "Wants better reporting" is not a moment; "the morning the invoice is queried and nobody can find the paperwork" is | Moesta |
| 1.2 | The channel must fit the **model**, not just the product. A channel/model misfit is not fixable with better copy, and trying is where quarters go | Balfour |
| 1.3 | Every channel decays. A channel working today is not a channel — ask what happens when the click-through halves | Chen |
| 1.4 | Demand you can serve is not demand that exists. Separate search *supply* from search *demand* before calling a term an opportunity | Fishkin |
| 1.5 | **Ask the category question of a real AI agent and record the literal answer.** If the product is absent from it, that absence is the finding | — |
| 1.6 | Name what gets fired. If nothing is displaced, there is no demand — only a capability (cross-ref `FF§1`) | Christensen |
| 1.7 | Name who the buyer is, and whether they are the user. When they differ, the door that reaches one may not reach the other | Verna |

## §2 — Message (S2 Learn)

*In eight seconds, what does the stranger believe this is?*

| # | Rule | Master |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | **Identify the awareness stage the copy is written for, and the stage the door actually delivers.** A mismatch is the finding, and it is usually the biggest one | Schwartz |
| 2.2 | Name the competitive alternative the reader has in mind — including "a confident colleague with an opinion". Positioning without a named alternative is decoration | Dunford |
| 2.3 | Every claim carries its proof, within reach of the claim. A claim whose evidence is one click away is an unproved claim | Hopkins |
| 2.4 | Specifics beat adjectives. Count the adjectives that would survive deletion without changing the meaning; that count is the finding | Ogilvy |
| 2.5 | Eight seconds on the page, then write down what you believe this is — **before** reading further. That sentence is the evidence | — |
| 2.6 | With no logo wall and no named customers, **evidence is the social proof**. Judge the substitute on its own terms, not the absence | — |
| 2.7 | A persuasion technique present without a matching truth is a defect, not a tactic. Cialdini read as a detector, not a license | Cialdini |

## §3 — Activation (S3 Sign up, S4 Onboard)

*How long until they hold something worth having — and does the
onboarding aim at it?*

| # | Rule | Master |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | **Stopwatch the time to first value.** Report wall-clock against the benchmark band, never as a rate (§0.8) | Bush |
| 3.2 | Count the steps between signup and the aha, and how many need a *decision* rather than an action. A flow that got shorter and harder got worse | — |
| 3.3 | **The empty state is the product** for a new account. Judge it as a designed screen, not as a fallback | — |
| 3.4 | Sample data that cannot become real data is a dead end, not an onramp. Name the path from the demo to their own | — |
| 3.5 | Onboarding tells the **user's** story, not the product's tour. A feature list with checkboxes is a tour | Hulick |
| 3.6 | Help must be reachable inside the step where they got stuck, not in a center they must leave the job to visit (cross-ref `FF§0.10`) | — |
| 3.7 | Every lifecycle email is walked as a **step in the flow**, not as an asset in a folder | — |
| 3.8 | Judge an email twice: does it **render and deliver** (any environment, preview path), and does the **sequence fire** (production only). A rendering pass is not a sequence pass | — |
| 3.9 | Name the aha before judging the onboarding. Onboarding with no named target cannot be evaluated, only described | Bush |

## §4 — Retention (S5 Use)

*Is there a reason to come back on Tuesday?*

| # | Rule | Master |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Name the reason to return. If the reason is a notification, judge the notification — an unsent one is not a reason | — |
| 4.2 | **Retention is the ceiling on growth.** A leak here caps every channel above it, so an S5 fail outranks any S1 recommendation | Winters |
| 4.3 | A paused recurring send is a **retention outage**, not a backlog item. Date it and say what it has cost since | — |
| 4.4 | What happens when they go quiet? A product with no dormant path has no win-back, and silence is its most common state | Chen |
| 4.5 | Judge whether a returning user finds anything **changed since last time**. Same screen, same numbers, no reason to have come | — |
| 4.6 | The curve must flatten. A cohort that never levels off is churn with a delay, and no acquisition rate outruns it | Winters |

## §5 — Revenue (S6 Pay)

*Is the moment they are asked for money the moment they would agree?*

| # | Rule | Master |
|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | **The ask lands at the moment value was felt**, not the moment a limit was hit. These are rarely the same moment, and only the first one converts | — |
| 5.2 | A limit the user meets **before** the aha is a wall, not a gate. Walls are charged for access; gates are charged for more | — |
| 5.3 | The published price and the in-app price agree, field for field — plan names, limits, what each tier includes | — |
| 5.4 | The payment leg is walked to **completion in test mode**. Never a real charge. If a test environment is ever found holding a live key, stop and report at severity 4 (§M.10) | — |
| 5.5 | Name what the free tier is *for*. A free tier that cannot reach the aha is a demo; one that never needs to be left is the product | Verna |

## §6 — Loops (S7 Grow)

*Does one person using this produce another person using this?*

| # | Rule | Master |
|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | **The loop test.** Does this stage's output re-enter the top of the funnel? `compounding` is unavailable without a yes, and the yes must name the path | Balfour |
| 6.2 | **Draw the loop before judging it.** An undrawable loop does not exist — and drawing it usually reveals which arrow is missing | Balfour |
| 6.3 | Name every outward-facing surface: anything that reaches a person who is not the user. Those are the only candidates for a loop | Chen |
| 6.4 | Expansion and acquisition are different loops and never share a verdict | Verna |
| 6.5 | A funnel improvement that does not feed a loop is a **one-off**, and must be labelled one. One-offs are legitimate; mislabelling them as growth is not | Balfour |

## §7 — Blueprint & method

*How the walk is conducted. Cross-cutting: cited alongside a domain rule,
not instead of one.*

| # | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 7.1 | **Every finding names its backstage cause.** Frontstage-only is a symptom. Which job, which router, which table, which cron, which env var — a recommendation with an empty backstage row is not ready | Shostack, via Kalbach |
| 7.2 | Name the moments of truth before walking, and weight the walk toward them. Five to ten moments carry most of the outcome; grading thirty touchpoints evenly produces a report nobody finishes | — |
| 7.3 | **Walk it, never infer it.** A step count read off routes is a guess, and a guess that looks like evidence is worse than a gap | §M.4 |
| 7.4 | State the environment for every leg, per `fresh.config.md`. Say so per leg and flag any leg that ran elsewhere | §M.4 |
| 7.5 | Every fail quotes what actually happened — the literal string, the literal count, the literal screen. Unquoted fails are discarded | §M.5 |
| 7.6 | Hand visual density, hierarchy and restraint findings to `fresh-design-review`; hand in-product job-flow findings to `fresh-job-walk`. Do not duplicate their work | §M.12 |

## The line of visibility

Every stage is walked in two rows, and this is not optional:

- **Frontstage** — what the stranger sees, reads, receives, clicks, waits for.
- **Backstage** — the process that was supposed to produce it.

Most products are many autonomous workflows behind a handful of human
touchpoints — record the counts in `fresh.config.md`. A walk that records
only the frontstage reports symptoms and hands over a debugging job
(§7.1).

## Benchmarks — the stopwatch

Per **§0.8**, these are compared against an **observed** walk and never
used to compute a rate the product cannot measure (§0.4). *"17 minutes to
first value, against a 5-minute best-in-class"* is a finding. *"Our
activation rate is 30%"* is not available (§0.3).

**The bands themselves have no single authoritative source** — they are
ranges that recur across published vendor benchmarks, which is weaker
provenance than anything else here. `references/activation.md` says so at
length; do not cite them as though they were measured.

| Measure | Band |
|---|---|
| **Time to value**, self-serve B2B | **<5 min** excellent — where the best-known design and productivity tools sit · **5–20 min** typical, acceptable · **20–60 min** loses a meaningful share before value · **>60 min** needs assisted onboarding to hold conversion |
| Reaching the aha at all | ~**two-thirds** of SaaS users never do |
| B2B activation target | **30–40%** reach the aha within 7 days |

## The verdict scale

One word per stage, from this fixed scale:

`broken` · `leaking` · `working` · `earning` · `compounding`

**`compounding` is reserved and has a test** (§6.1): a stage only
compounds if its output re-enters the top of the funnel. S7 is judged as
a loop rather than a stage — the one place the funnel framing is
deliberately abandoned.

**Nothing is scored** (§0.6, §M.6). Severity 0–4 lives on findings, never
on the system.
